Gig money journey

What a month looks like for someone paid through PayPal by several apps and clients, with an assistant that keeps the ledger, puts tax aside and chases what they're owed. Nothing goes out to a client until they approve it. Amounts and clients on this page are examples.

PayPal row: the API or webhook doing the work Approval gate planned not built yet

Who it's for

WorkerA multi-app freelancer in the North of England: delivery shifts, a small craft shop and the odd photography job. All of it lands in one PayPal business account.
SituationForty payments a month from five sources. No idea what was earned this quarter, how much belongs to HMRC, or which client still owes for last month.
What they needOne clear number they can spend, tax set aside before it's spent, and invoices that go out and get paid without an evening of admin.

Worker does Finishes a shift, sells a print, delivers a photo set. Opens the app. Nothing to type in. Checks the "safe to spend" figure before buying something. Types invoice Northside Café £120 for Saturday's shoot, due in 14 days. Reads the draft. Approves, edits the amount, or rejects it. Sees a nudge that an invoice is 5 days overdue. Downloads the month's summary for their records.
PayPal does Captures the payment: Orders v2 create and capture (seeded in the sandbox for the demo). Sends PAYMENT.CAPTURE.COMPLETED by webhook; the signature is checked with verify-webhook-signature. Is the source of truth for every amount and fee in the ledger. Nothing yet: the draft is local. Invoicing v2 create, then send, only after approval. Invoicing v2 remind; INVOICING.INVOICE.PAID arrives by webhook when the client pays. Transaction history to cross-check the ledger planned
Assistant does — Adds the payment to the ledger and tags its source (delivery app, shop, client) from the payer and note. Keeps a running tax set-aside estimate and shows what's left to spend. Turns the sentence into a draft invoice through the PayPal Agent Toolkit, matching the client from past payments. Stops. Nothing goes to the client until the worker approves. Drafts a polite reminder and suggests sending it; it's sent only on approval. Writes a plain-language summary: earned, set aside, owed, paid late.

Without the app Payments pile up in PayPal next to refunds and transfers. Scrolls the activity list and copies amounts into a spreadsheet, when they remember. Spends it all, then gets a tax bill in January they hadn't saved for. Builds an invoice by hand in PayPal or a template, late at night. An auto-send tool would email the client with no check. Doesn't chase, because it's awkward. The money arrives late or never. Sorts a year of payments by hand before Self Assessment.

Approve and sendEditReject

Approve

The invoice is created and sent through PayPal Invoicing. It shows as sent and turns paid when the webhook arrives.

Edit or reject

Nothing reaches the client. An edit returns to the draft; a rejection is logged with the reason.

Guardrails

Sandbox only. The assistant can draft and suggest, but every call that contacts a client needs a click. The tax figure is always labelled an estimate.

How money and invoices flow

sequenceDiagram
    actor W as Worker
    participant App as Assistant app
    participant AI as Model + Agent Toolkit
    participant PP as PayPal sandbox
    PP->>App: PAYMENT.CAPTURE.COMPLETED (webhook)
    App->>PP: verify-webhook-signature
    App->>AI: payer, amount, note
    AI-->>App: source tag
    App-->>W: ledger row + safe to spend
    W->>App: "invoice Northside Café £120..."
    App->>AI: request + past payers
    AI-->>App: draft invoice
    App-->>W: draft card
    alt approve
        W->>App: approve
        App->>PP: create + send invoice
        PP->>App: INVOICING.INVOICE.PAID (webhook)
        App-->>W: marked paid
    else edit or reject
        W->>App: change or reason
        App-->>W: nothing sent
    end
