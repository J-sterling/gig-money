Gig Money Architecture

PayPal money assistant for gig workers, built for the PayPal AI Hackathon. Payments arrive from PayPal by webhook and go into a ledger. The AI tags each one and drafts invoices, and nothing goes to a client until the worker approves it.

Built and tested (25 Oct) Planned for the submission (12 Nov) Optional or after 12 Nov

One payment and one invoice, step by step

  1. A visitor gets their own demo workspace, seeded with a made-up month of income.
  2. "Get paid" creates a sandbox order tagged with the workspace (custom_id). The visitor approves it as the sandbox buyer.
  3. On return, the app captures the order and records the payment from PayPal's reply. No public URL is needed for this.
  4. PayPal also sends PAYMENT.CAPTURE.COMPLETED. The app checks the signature with PayPal, and the same capture is never recorded twice.
  5. The tax set-aside and "safe to spend" figures update, labelled as an estimate.
  6. The worker types an invoice request, and the model returns a schema-checked draft. Planned: the draft waits at the approval gate; on approval, PayPal Invoicing creates and sends it, and INVOICING.INVOICE.PAID marks it paid.

Non-functional requirements that shape the design

  • Sandbox only. The app won't start unless PAYPAL_ENV=sandbox.
  • Only real PayPal events. Every webhook signature is checked with PayPal; unverified or duplicate events never touch the ledger.
  • Approval is enforced by the server. Create, send and remind run only after an approval call, and every decision is recorded.
  • Runs from the README. Fresh clone to a working ledger in about 15 minutes, with no database or Docker, on Node 22 or newer.
  • Feels instant. Webhook answered in under 2 s with AI tagging done afterwards; ledger loads in under 1 s; an invoice draft in under 10 s.
  • Can't run up bills. Daily and per-draft caps on model calls, inside the monthly budget.
